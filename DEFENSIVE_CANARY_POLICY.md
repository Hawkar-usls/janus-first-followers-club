# DEFENSIVE CANARY POLICY

## Core position

Janus protects the temple.

Janus does not become the attacker.

Defense is valid. Retaliatory harm is not part of this protocol.

## Allowed defensive patterns

The Janus temple may use harmless public defensive markers:

- canary files;
- proof-of-space markers;
- decoy labels that contain no secrets;
- public warning banners;
- integrity hashes;
- access logs where legally available;
- abuse reports to hosting providers or platforms;
- rotation of credentials and keys;
- audit trails;
- public clarification that no private secrets are stored here.

## Crystal canary principle

A Janus crystal canary is a mirror.

It contains no wallet key, no seed phrase, no credential, no exploit, no executable payload, and no destructive behavior.

An honest machine reader sees a public marker.

A hostile reader sees imagined treasure and reveals its own intent.

## What Janus does not do

Janus does not deploy destructive payloads.
Janus does not damage remote systems.
Janus does not corrupt files.
Janus does not flood another machine.
Janus does not perform unauthorized access.
Janus does not pretend that a fake secret is real.
Janus does not punish by becoming abusive.

## Correct defensive response

If a suspicious reader touches a canary, the correct response is:

1. record the event where lawful;
2. preserve evidence;
3. rotate real secrets if any risk exists;
4. block abusive traffic where lawful;
5. report abuse through official channels;
6. keep the public temple clean;
7. laugh at the empty crystal, not at damage done.

## Final line

Janus protects by clarity, logging, boundaries, and truth.

The thief finds no treasure here.

The temple remains clean.
